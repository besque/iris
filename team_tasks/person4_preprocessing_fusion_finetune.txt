PERSON 4: IMAGE PLUMBING + THE MANDATORY FINE-TUNE + OPTICAL-SAR FUSION
========================================================================
Read team_tasks/README.txt first for the big picture. You have the 5070 Ti.

CONTEXT: WHY YOUR ROLE EXISTS
-----------------------------
Three jobs, all critical:
1. PLUMBING: real satellite data comes as GeoTIFF files with 1 to 13 bands,
   16-bit values, and geo-metadata. The VLM tools eat normal RGB images.
   Someone must read GeoTIFFs, figure out if an image is optical or SAR,
   check that a pair actually matches, and convert everything to clean RGB.
   Everyone downstream depends on this.
2. THE FINE-TUNE: the rules DISQUALIFY any solution without at least one
   model adapted on remote-sensing data (BigEarthNet named specifically).
   You produce that proof: a training script, a checkpoint, and
   before/after numbers. This is the single most important compliance item.
3. FUSION: the optical + SAR joint analysis tool (mandatory task). SAR is
   radar imaging: works through clouds and at night. Water looks very dark
   in SAR (smooth surfaces bounce the radar away); buildings look very
   bright (corners bounce it straight back). Optical gives color/vegetation.
   Combining them finds water and built-up areas more reliably than either
   alone, exactly ISRO's monsoon-flood use case.

TASK 1 (MINIMUM, FIRST, ~2-3 hrs): the validator + converter.
-------------------------------------------------------------
backend/preprocessing/validator.py, implement validate_inputs(file_paths):
  a) Open each file. rasterio for .tif/.tiff, PIL for .png/.jpg.
  b) Detect modality, simple heuristics are fine:
       1-2 bands + float/16-bit + high speckle -> SAR
       3 bands 8-bit -> optical RGB
       4+ bands -> multispectral (optical)
     Also accept filename hints ("s1", "sar", "vv", "vh" -> SAR).
     Record HOW you decided in the warnings list so it is auditable.
  c) Pairs: same pixel size? If both have geo-metadata (CRS + bounds via
     rasterio), do the bounds overlap? If sizes differ slightly, resize the
     second to the first and add a warning. If they clearly do not overlap
     geographically, return a hard error string, do not crash.
  d) Classify: one image -> single_optical | single_sar
     two images, one SAR one optical -> crossmodal_pair
     two images, same modality      -> bitemporal_pair
     Return {"config_type": ..., "images": [...], "warnings": [...]}.
     These exact names are already wired into the controller.
  e) to_rgb(path) -> uint8 HxWx3 numpy array:
     multispectral: pick RGB bands (Sentinel-2: B4,B3,B2), percentile
     stretch 2-98% to 0-255. SAR: log-scale (10*log10), clip, stretch,
     replicate to 3 channels. Persons 2/3 call this; ship it early, rough
     is fine.
  f) Add 2-3 tests in tests/test_validator.py (a fake 1-band tif made with
     rasterio in-memory counts). Announce in the group when done.

TASK 2 (MINIMUM, start by early afternoon): the BigEarthNet fine-tune.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal is honest, documented adaptation, not a giant training run.
  a) Data: do NOT download full BigEarthNet (hundreds of GB). Get a subset:
     torchgeo's BigEarthNet loader supports it, and HuggingFace hosts
     subsets, search "BigEarthNet" on HF datasets. A few thousand patches
     with their multi-label classes (e.g. "arable land", "urban fabric",
     "water bodies") is plenty. Put the download script in scripts/.
  b) Model: RemoteCLIP (https://github.com/ChenDelong1999/RemoteCLIP) or
     plain OpenCLIP ViT-B/32. CLIP = a model matching images to text.
  c) Train: LoRA (the `peft` library) on image-text pairs where the text is
     the label names ("a satellite image of urban fabric and water bodies").
     Batch 64+, 2-3 epochs, an hour or two on the 5070 Ti. Script goes in
     training/finetune_clip.py with a config file so it is reproducible.
  d) PROOF (this is the deliverable): zero-shot label accuracy on a held-out
     split, BEFORE vs AFTER fine-tuning. Even 61% -> 74% is a great slide.
     Write numbers + command to evaluation/results/adaptation.md.
  e) BONUS built in: train on both Sentinel-2 (optical) AND Sentinel-1
     (SAR) patches from BigEarthNet and your model understands both
     modalities, which Task 3 then uses. That makes OUR OWN fine-tuned
     model the fusion engine, a strong story for judges.

TASK 3 (MINIMUM, after 1, alongside 2): the fusion tool.
--------------------------------------------------------
backend/tools/fusion/fusion_tool.py -> class FusionTool(Tool), pattern in
backend/tools/mocks.py, input_types = ["crossmodal_pair"].
Bare-minimum version, classical + honest (no ML needed to work):
  a) WATER mask: SAR backscatter below a threshold (water is dark in SAR).
     If the optical is multispectral with a near-infrared band, also compute
     NDWI (a standard water index: (Green-NIR)/(Green+NIR), high = water)
     and AND the two masks. Agreement of two sensors = higher confidence.
  b) BUILT-UP mask: SAR very bright + optical not vegetation.
  c) ToolResult:
     text       = "Water covers ~X% (SAR + optical agree on Y% of it),
                   built-up ~Z%, concentrated in the north."
     spatial    = {"type": "mask", "data": <label mask: 0 none,1 water,2 built>}
     confidence = fraction of mask area where both sensors agreed
     metadata   = {"model": "sar_optical_rules_v1", "thresholds": {...}}
BETTER version, once Task 2 finishes: classify tiles of both images with
your fine-tuned CLIP ("water bodies" vs "urban fabric" vs other) and merge
with the classical masks. Now the mandatory fine-tune IS the fusion brain.
